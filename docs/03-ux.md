# 03 UX

## Information architecture

Four tabs at the bottom. Games, Find, List, You.

```
Games   the default. Multi-game tab strip on top, one board below
Find    packs to play, friends' public games, pack search
List    the Life List, everything you have ever found
You     profile, stats, achievements, friends, settings
```

Chat, a news feed, and a shop are deliberately absent. Anything that makes the
app worth opening when you are not outside works against the point of it.

## The multi-game model

The brief asks for a tabular structure across simultaneous games. On a phone
the right pattern is a horizontally scrolling chip strip pinned under the
header, behaving like browser tabs.

Each chip carries:

- a filled dot in the game's accent colour
- a short name, truncated to about 14 characters
- a badge when something happened since you last looked, showing a count
- a thin ring when you are one square from a bingo

The accent colour is the load-bearing part. Six parallel games all called
something like "Spring 2027" are indistinguishable by text at chip width, but
a yellow one and a blue one are not. Assign the accent at creation from the
palette, never repeating a colour among a player's active games until all
seven are used.

Swiping left and right on the board moves between games. This is safe because
the grid is tap-only, with no horizontal gesture of its own. Past roughly six
games the strip gets an "All" chip that opens a sheet with the full list,
sorted by recent activity.

## Screens

### Games, board view

The default screen and the one that has to be perfect.

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Springo                      ⚙︎      │
│  ●Ozark Spring ②  ●Road Trip  ●Birds │   chip strip, scrolls
├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Ozark Spring Ephemerals             │
│  You  13 marks  ·  Sarah  11 marks   │   score line, tap to swap boards
├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                      │
│   ▢  ▣  ▢  ▣  ▢                      │
│   ▣  ▢  ▣  ▢  ▢                      │   the board, 5 x 5
│   ▢  ▣  ★  ▢  ▣                      │   ★ free space
│   ▣  ▢  ▢  ▣  ▢                      │
│   ▢  ▣  ▢  ▢  ▣                      │
│                                      │
├──────────────────────────────────────┤
│  📷 Photo mode  ·  ⚡ 2 marks queued  │   sync state, only when queued
└──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Square contents: item name, two lines maximum, centred, with a small emoji or
icon above it when the pack supplies one. Marked squares fill with the game
accent and the label goes to white. In photo mode a marked square shows the
photo as the fill, dimmed, with the label over it. That single detail turns the
board into a collage by the end of a game and it is the best free thing in the
design.

Tapping a square opens its detail sheet. In honor mode an unmarked square can
also be marked with a single tap on the face; in photo mode the tap always opens
the sheet, because the camera is the next step anyway.

**The detail sheet** carries the item name, its scientific name, the
identification hint, a licensed reference photo, and your own photo beside it at
the same size. That pairing is what makes an opponent's mark checkable without a
dispute mechanism. Unmark lives here behind a two-tap confirm, because a mis-tap
that erases a real sighting from three weeks ago is the worst thing this app can
do. Reference photos never appear on the square face itself; the reasoning is in
[08-reference-photos.md](08-reference-photos.md).

### Board switching

Tapping a player in the score line slides their board in from the side with the
same layout and a header that clearly says whose it is. The visual difference
has to be unmistakable, not subtle: a tinted background wash and a persistent
"Sarah's board" pill. A player who marks a square on someone else's board
because it looked like theirs will be furious, and so will their opponent.

Their squares show their marks and, in photo mode, their photos. Long-press
gives the reaction row. Nothing on this screen can change the game state.

### Create a game

Four steps, each a single screen. Never a form.

1. **Theme.** A text field with a live suggestion list, and a shelf of curated
   packs underneath. Typing "Arkansas spring" should surface the existing pack
   before it offers to generate a new one, because playing an existing pack is
   better for everyone.
2. **Review the list.** The generated items as chips, 30 to 40 of them, each
   with its hint line and its candidate reference photo. Tap to remove, hold to
   edit, plus to add your own, and a "next photo" control cycles through the
   other open-licensed candidates for that species. A counter shows how many are
   selected against the 24 needed. **This screen is the quality gate for the
   whole product.** Do not skip it, do not make it optional, do not put it
   behind an advanced toggle. Automated photo sourcing lands about 75 percent of
   images usable, and this minute of review is what takes it to 100.
3. **Rules.** Mode (honor or photo), board style, free space, win patterns, end
   date. The estimated bingo length recomputes live under the toggles.
4. **Invite.** Friend picker, plus a six character code and a share sheet link.
   The link should deep-link into the game and, for a user without the app,
   into a store page that returns them to the game after install.

The game starts when the creator says so, not when everyone has joined. Late
joiners get a board generated from the same seed and start behind, which is
fine.

### Life List

The quiet centre of the app.

A grid of everything you have ever marked, each with your best photo, the date
you first found it, and how many times you have found it since. Filterable by
pack, by season, by year. Tapping an entry shows every time you found it,
across every game, as a timeline.

Two things make it work:

- **It persists after the game is deleted.** The game is ephemeral, the record
  is not.
- **It has a count, and the count only goes up.** "247 things found" is a
  number people care about in a way that "14 games won" is not.

Add a share card: your list as a poster, one row per season. That is the
organic acquisition channel and it costs a day to build.

### Achievements

Thematic, tied to the observation loop, not to session counts. Eight for v1:

| Name | Condition |
|---|---|
| First Bloom | Mark your first square |
| Ephemeral | Bingo within 24 hours of a game starting |
| Slow Bloom | Win a game that ran 30 days or more |
| Dawn Patrol | Mark a square before 7am |
| Corner Store | Win by postage stamp |
| Full Bloom | Blackout a board |
| Herbarium | 100 photo-backed marks |
| Same Woods | 10 marks within 100m and 30 minutes of another player's mark in the same game |

Same Woods is the one worth building carefully. It quietly detects that two
people were actually out there together, which is the whole point of the app,
and nobody else has it. It needs coarse location on the mark, which means it
needs to be opt-in and the location must never be shown to another player, only
used for this comparison server-side at a 100m granularity.

Later additions should follow the same rule: reward what happened outside, not
how much the app was used.

### Stats

Per player: games played, games won, win rate, current streak, squares marked,
photo rate, fastest bingo, longest game, most-played pack, items on the Life
List, win pattern breakdown.

Per game, visible to all players: a small timeline of who marked what and when.
In a season-long game this is the thing people scroll through at the end, and
it is where the sentiment lives.

## Notifications

The most important surface, because the app is closed almost always.

**Send:**
- an opponent marked a square (batched, at most one per hour per game)
- someone got a bingo
- you were invited to a game
- a friend request
- one seasonal nudge: "Bloodroot usually blooms this week in your area"

**Never send:**
- "you are one away" (fires for a third of every game, see
  [02-game-rules.md](02-game-rules.md))
- "come back and play"
- streak-loss threats
- anything at all during a game with no recent activity

Copy should name the thing, not the action. "Sarah found Dutchman's Breeches"
beats "Sarah marked a square". The specific noun is what makes someone go
outside.

## Onboarding

Do not explain bingo. Do explain the three things that are not obvious:

1. you mark squares when you see the real thing, outside
2. the game runs for as long as it takes, there is no timer
3. it works with no signal, marks sync later

Three cards, skippable, then straight into a demo board seeded from a "things
near you right now" pack that can be genuinely completed in a walk around the
block. Finishing something in the first session is what converts.

Account creation should be deferred until the first game is created or joined.
Apple sign-in, Google sign-in, email. Handle picked at that point, with a
generated suggestion.

## Accessibility

- Colour is never the only marker. A marked square carries a check stroke as
  well as the accent fill, because the accent palette includes a red and a
  green that will read identically to a red-green colourblind player.
- Minimum square touch target 48dp. On a 5 by 5 grid at 375pt width with 8pt
  gaps, squares land near 62pt, which is comfortable.
- Every square is a labelled button for the screen reader: "Trout Lily, not
  marked, row 2 column 3". Announce a win as a live region.
- Reduce Motion collapses every animation to a 120ms cross fade. Required, and
  also a store review item.
- Dynamic Type up to the accessibility sizes. Square labels truncate to two
  lines with an ellipsis and the full name is in the detail sheet, so nothing
  is lost.
